SEGMENT 10:00 – 0:08

What Gibbs-energy minimization gives you

In

Temperature, pressure, and moles of each element.

Not compounds. You give it Li, Be, F — not "LiF" — and the minimizer decides what forms.

Out

Which phases exist, how much of each, and the speciation inside them.

For a salt, the liquid is a SUBQ/MQMQA phase — reported as cation/anion fractions and quadruplets, not a simple species list.

  • It is equilibrium only. No kinetics, no transport, no release rate — the thermodynamic end state and driving force, which is exactly what a transport model needs.
  • That transport model is next hour's MOOSE coupling. Today stays entirely on standalone Thermochimica + MSTDB.

The chemical potential trap

2 species (LiF, BeF₂) span 3 elements (Li, Be, F). The system is one equation short — individual element potentials are mathematically under-determined.

SEGMENT 30:16 – 0:28

The rule to take home

  • Individual potentials swing 20–40 kJ/mol on a composition change of 1 part in 400,000.
  • Species combinations are invariant to <0.1 kJ/mol. μ_Li+μ_F and μ_Be+2μ_F stay fixed no matter how you break the tie.
  • Never report or couple a bare element chemical potential out of a stoichiometric salt calculation.
①
Use the species-level combination
②
Perturb one input mass slightly
③
fuzzy = .TRUE. — reproduces ② exactly

Caveat: fuzzy stoichiometry can introduce tiny spurious trace phases — inspect your output.

SEGMENT 40:28 – 0:40

MQMQA/SUBQ JSON — structural gotchas

  • No "species" key. MSFL reports "cations" / "anions" / "quadruplets" instead — a naive ['species'] lookup raises KeyError.
  • Names truncated to 8 characters. U[CN=VII (should be U[CN=VII]), Be[Dimer (should be Be[Dimer]).
  • Dimers count double. U[Dimer] is a U₂ unit — weight its mole fraction ×2 when summing total U content.
print(list(d['1']['solution phases']['MSFL'].keys()))

SEGMENT 70:58 – 1:00

Four things to take with you

  • Elements in, phases + speciation out. You don't tell it "LiF" — you give it Li and F.
  • Never trust a bare element potential in a stoichiometric salt. Use species combinations, perturbation, or fuzzy.
  • The halide mass balance sets redox; redox sets corrosion — quantitatively, not qualitatively. ~9 orders of magnitude for Cr across a realistic F window.
  • This is equilibrium only. Rates and transport come from the MOOSE coupling — next hour.
